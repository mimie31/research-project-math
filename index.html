📖 Food Security Scenario (Onion Supply Chain)

Malaysia is currently expanding domestic bulb onion production to reduce reliance on foreign imports. The most pressing food security challenge within this agricultural logistics chain is **cold-chain weight loss** driven by the transpiration and respiration of stored onions.

Cumulative water loss maps onto a sigmoidal logistical curve \(f(x)\). By applying the **First Derivative \(f'(x)\)**, we evaluate the instantaneous daily rate of moisture loss measured in **g/kg/day**.

When this water loss velocity reaches its peak (**apex inflection point**), the internal cellular walls of the onions break down. This exposes the entire stockpile to pathogenic mold infestations (*Aspergillus niger*), causing devastating post-harvest losses before produce ever reaches consumer markets.

🧮 Post-Harvest Decay Model

Cumulative Moisture Loss \(f(x)\) (grams/kg):

$$f(x) = \frac{L_{max}}{1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}$$

Derivative \(f'(x)\) (Instantaneous Daily Loss Velocity):

$$f'(x) = \frac{L_{max} \cdot \beta \cdot (k \cdot \eta) \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}{(1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x})^2}$$
\(x\) = Storage Timeline (0 to 60 Days)
\(L_{max}\) = Max Water Loss Capacity (150g/kg)
\(\beta\) = Initial Curing Scale Parameter (45)
\(k\) = Respiratory Constant (0.15)
\(\eta\) = Humidity & Temperature Control Factor (0.6 to 1.4)

Deep-Dive: How Calculus Controls Food Supply Chains

1. The Critical Transpiration Apex

The mathematical peak of the first derivative curve \(f'(x)\) represents the highest transpiration velocity. This occurs at the curve's inflection point where the second derivative equals zero (\(f''(x) = 0\)). If a facility fails to ramp up ventilation during this acceleration window, stagnant air pockets spark rapid crop rot.

2. Controlling Relative Humidity (RH)

Manipulating the **Atmospheric Stress Index (\(\eta\))** simulates infrastructure conditions. Hot, unventilated rooms drop efficiency (\(\eta \to 1.4\)), making the curve rise steeply and narrowing shelf-life window. Advanced climate-controlled facilities suppress \(\eta\), flattening the rate derivative and buffering supply.

3. Strategic Distribution Windows

By pinpointing where the asymptotic ceiling of function \(f(x)\) begins to plateau, logistics analysts forecast distribution dead-lines. This helps move supply out of collection points and onto market retail shelves before water loss causes crops to drop beneath marketable FAMA grade standards.
