Aquaculture Biomass Rates via Calculus Derivatives

Using the first derivative \(f'(x)\) to optimize Feed Conversion Ratios (FCR) in Malaysian Black Tiger Shrimp farming.

🦐 The Bioscience Problem: When do we harvest?

In Malaysian coastal aquaculture (e.g., Kuala Selangor or Tawau), feeds constitute over 60% of total operational costs. Overfeeding late in the cycle results in toxic ammonia accumulation, water degradation, and financial loss, threatening local food security. By tracking the instantaneous growth rate—the first derivative \(f'(x)\) of the individual weight function—biologists identify the precise week where growth velocity peaks and begins to decline. Harvesting near this peak efficiency ensures maximum protein yield with minimal ecological and economic waste.

Data Reference Framework: Parameters adapted from the growth kinetics of commercial Penaeus monodon stock profiles recorded under the Department of Fisheries Malaysia (Official Portal: www.dof.gov.my).

Biomass Growth Function \(f(x)\)

Predicts average individual shrimp weight (grams) over time \(x\) (weeks):

$$f(x) = 45 \cdot e^{-e^{-0.22(x - 9)}}$$

A Gompertz Sigmoid model where 45g is the targeted market asymptotic weight, and 9 weeks represents the inflection point of maximum initial velocity.

First Derivative \(f'(x)\) (Growth Velocity)

Calculates exact rate of mass accumulation (grams/week) at instance \(x\):

$$f'(x) = 9.9 \cdot e^{-0.22(x - 9) - e^{-0.22(x - 9)}}$$

Obtained analytically using the Chain Rule. It reveals the exact speed at which the shrimp is gaining tissue mass at any chosen week.
