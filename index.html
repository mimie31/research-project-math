📖 The Bioscience Scenario

In intensive bulb onion storage management, moisture loss is non-linear and conforms to a sigmoidal (S-shaped) logistic curve. Understanding the instantaneous rate of change—the **First Derivative \(f'(x)\)**—is critical.

The derivative represents the daily average water loss (grams/kg/day). In agricultural logistics, identifying when the decay rate peaks is vital. Once the derivative peaks and begins to decline, the bulb's structural integrity decreases rapidly.

Keeping onions past this peak increases supply risk dramatically, as rot represents nearly 70% of total post-harvest storage losses in tropical climates like Malaysia.

📊 Mathematical Growth Model

Cumulative Loss \(f(x)\):

$$f(x) = \frac{W_{max}}{1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}$$

Derivative \(f'(x)\) (Daily Growth Rate):

$$f'(x) = \frac{W_{max} \cdot \beta \cdot (k \cdot \eta) \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x}}{(1 + \beta \cdot e^{-k \cdot \eta \cdot x})^2}$$
\(x\) = Day of Storage cycle (0 to 42)
\(W_{max}\) = Target asymptotic loss (g)
\(\beta\) = Scaling parameter (fixed at 55)
\(k\) = Biological decay coefficient
\(\eta\) = Housing Efficiency Index (0.7 to 1.15)

Deep-Dive: How Calculus Solves Poultry Economics

1 The Biological Peak

The peak of the derivative curve \(f'(x)\) occurs at the inflection point of the cumulative weight curve \(f(x)\). Mathematically, this is where the second derivative equals zero: \(f''(x) = 0\). Practically, this marks the maximum possible growth efficiency. After this day, the rate of weight accumulation slows down.

2 The Microclimate Equation

In tropical Malaysia, temperatures regularly exceed 30°C. In the simulator, the Housing Efficiency index (\(\eta\)) represents the microclimate parameter. In an open-house system (\(\eta \approx 0.75\)), birds suffer heat stress, slowing metabolic enzyme processes, shifting the inflection point to the right.

3 Deciding the Harvest Date

Broilers are usually harvested in Malaysia between days 35 to 42. By cross-referencing feed cost per day, farmers use the derivative curve to locate the point where profit margins are maximized. Harvesting on day 35 meets standard wet-market requirements, while day 42 caters to downstream portions.
